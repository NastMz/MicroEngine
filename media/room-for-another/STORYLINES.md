# Storylines, second film

Why a second film: "Seated" (`../seated`) opened on code that draws a pixel star and gave ten seconds of
full-frame play to four 2D demos, so the picture said "pixel-art 2D engine" whatever the words said. It also
swapped a module under a running game, which reads as hot swap.

The one thing: *MicroEngine is a kernel with sockets. What a game is made of is a choice of modules: the ones
that ship, others, your own, or ones nobody has built.*

## A. Room for another (built)

- **Device and form:** the object is the explanation, as a flight through one world. The brand's board becomes a
  structure in space, a lintel on walls, and the whole film is modules arriving in its bays.
- **What the viewer sees:** an empty structure; modules slide in, one per shot; a screen on top comes on; then four
  other configurations, each a new shot built before its screen lights; then the structure grows new bays; then it
  turns to the front, flattens and is the mark, and the amber module seats.
- **First two seconds:** a close view of empty bays in depth, and a block sliding into one on the second beat.
- **Why it answers the brief:** the picture is dimensional and abstract, so no art style defines it; games are small
  evidence on a screen; replacing is shown as building another configuration, never as a change under a running game;
  3D is an outline that never seats, tagged "NOT BUILT YET".
- **Sound:** the score is assembled like the board: kick, hats and bass each arrive with a module.

## B. Seated, corrected

- Keep the first film and repair it: replace the star with the platform catalog code, shrink the montage, turn the
  swap into two side-by-side boards.
- **Why not:** the hook would still be code and the middle still a montage; the positioning problem is in the
  structure of that film, not in its details.

## C. One game, many engines

- **Device and form:** split screen to one. The same game on a wall of screens, each above a different set of
  modules, collapsing into one board.
- **Why not:** it makes one 2D game the hero of every frame, the opposite of the brief.
