# Storylines, second film

The second film tells the architecture. Its first attempt, "Room for another", drew the mark's board as a structure
of walls and bays with blocks sliding in: the same socket logic as "Seated", given depth. The owner's note: it read
as a 3D reinterpretation of the first film. The idea stays; the picture is new.

The brief: an assembly zone. Pieces gather, assemble into a configuration, and a game comes of it; the configuration
is taken apart, some pieces change, it is assembled again, and another game comes of it.

The one thing: *one kernel; a game is built on a configuration of pieces, and another game on another.*

## A. Assembly (built)

- **Device and form:** the object is the explanation, as one frame that rebuilds. An exploded drawing on a table:
  solids float along dashed guides and close around a kernel, three times, into three different objects.
- **Why 3D:** an exploded view needs depth to mean anything; pieces dock on six sides and stack.
- **What keeps it honest:** the plane with a game appears only when a configuration is complete and is gone before
  anything is undone, so nothing changes under a running game. The two games are two different games. The third
  plane is empty: what is built with your own pieces is not ours to show.
- **Look:** warm paper and a table with registration marks; the kernel in ink; family colours on pieces.
- **Sound:** 150 BPM, faster and more mechanical than the other two; a note per arriving piece, a run of eighths
  while pieces dock, a different melody for each game.

## B. The workbench

- **Device and form:** a top-down technical table, 2.5D, pieces laid out flat like a parts tray and pushed together.
- **Why not:** flat pieces on a flat table read as a jigsaw, and a jigsaw has one solution.

## C. Orbit

- **Device and form:** the kernel as a centre with pieces in orbit, captured into rings that are the configurations.
- **Why not:** orbits and rings are decoration; nothing in the engine goes round anything.
